# Page strategy · Seasonal and Illness Care

**Approved 26 November 2026** · Option A, access-first with on-site testing in the hero. This strategy governs the Seasonal and Illness Care page. General Pediatrics has a separate page and a different visitor goal.

## Objective and search intent

Turn paid-search visits into sick-visit phone inquiries. The primary CTA is a call to the number for the selected office. The secondary CTA opens the GoHighLevel (GHL) appointment request popup. Use the practice's main number, **(847) 295-1220**, while tracking numbers are pending.

The visitor is likely a Lake County caregiver with a child who is sick now. Their main question is “Can my child be seen near me, and what do I do now?” They may also want to know whether testing is available, whether they can come without an appointment, and whom they can call after hours. The page should give them enough confidence to take the next appropriate step, which may be a call, a visit during the applicable sick-call window, or emergency care.

**Approved primary theme** · Local pediatric sick visits. Same-day availability is a qualified benefit. On-site flu, RSV, and strep testing is a supporting theme and belongs on the first screen. Search intent is inferred. The brief supplies no actual campaign terms, ad copy, performance data, or promotional offer. Phrases such as “pediatric sick visit near me” are unvalidated AI candidates. Once the ads are available, verify that the office city, visit availability, testing scope, and call action agree from click to page. The offer is access to pediatric sick care.

Use this page for testing searches when the visitor wants a pediatric evaluation with testing as appropriate. Brand/portal and preventive vaccination searches need other destinations. General Pediatrics should carry practice-selection and well-care intent. Emergency care, broad urgent-care or all-hours walk-in expectations, and standalone testing need separate evaluation before targeting. Confirm new-patient eligibility before opening non-brand acquisition.

## Conversion path and section order

Keep the overall page compact to medium length. The hero and access panel satisfy the immediate need. Clinical detail stays brief, and FAQs absorb the remaining logistical questions.

### 1. Hero · Local pediatric sick visits

- **Purpose and visitor question** · Establish paid-search relevance and enable an immediate call. “Is this the right place for my sick child, and can I contact them now?”
- **Main message and content** · Lead with the service and selected office city. Put same-day appointments when available and on-site flu, RSV, and strep testing directly below. Give Morning Sick Call a short cue and leave the schedule details to the next section.
- **Emotion and trust** · Acknowledge the parent's need for direction. Use one compact credential, such as board-certified pediatricians or 50+ years serving Lake County. Reserve a discreet Google badge for verified data for the corresponding office.
- **Visual role and treatment** · The strongest type hierarchy sits against a white background. Use one approved image if it adds warmth or authenticity. On mobile, service, city, testing, and call come before the image.
- **CTA and transitions** · Put Call first and Request Appointment second. Continue the ad's office and service promise, then point to the access panel for the visit routes.

### 2. Getting seen · Morning Sick Call, phone, and emergency guidance

- **Purpose and visitor question** · Remove arrival and scheduling uncertainty. “Do I need an appointment, and which route applies to me?”
- **Main message and content** · Explain 8–9 a.m. Morning Sick Call and website check-in from the office parking lot. Publish Monday–Friday for all three offices and Saturday for Lake Bluff and Lindenhurst, based on the practice's location pages. Explain calling for a sick appointment outside the morning window and 24/7 phone support for guidance. Include a visible practice-sourced 911/ER callout here with wording approved for publication.
- **Emotion and trust** · Give caregivers a concrete first step. Exact office hours and a clear emergency route support confidence.
- **Visual role and treatment** · Use a pale-blue panel with two distinct routes, stacked on mobile. Keep the emergency guidance in a separate, easily scanned callout beside or directly after the access explanation.
- **CTA and transitions** · Repeat Call after the access explanation. Add a tertiary link to Morning Sick Call check-in only with approval and clear eligibility. Follow the hero's access cue with practical steps, then explain what a visit can help clarify.

### 3. Common illnesses and on-site testing

- **Purpose and visitor question** · Explain what the visit can do. “Can you help with these symptoms and test for flu, RSV, or strep?”
- **Main message and content** · Cover common cold-like symptoms and seasonal illness in a short list. Explain that a clinician evaluates the child and determines whether a test is appropriate. Testing can help guide next steps. Keep any result timing qualified by test type.
- **Emotion and trust** · Replace uncertainty with a realistic idea of what to expect. Clinical judgment and named test capabilities are the proof.
- **Visual role and treatment** · Use a quiet, primarily text-led section. Use one compact test list or comparison if it makes the information easier to scan.
- **CTA and transitions** · The call remains available via the sticky controls. Move from how to get seen to what happens at the visit and then introduce the people who provide care.

### 4. Pediatric team and parent proof

- **Purpose and visitor question** · Build confidence in the practice and show relevant parent experience. “Who will care for my child, and what have other families experienced?”
- **Main message and content** · Use board-certified pediatricians and 50+ years serving Lake County. Use the dedicated Google Reviews section for verified local rating, count, and selected reviews, with a link to the corresponding profile.
- **Emotion and trust** · Give the clinical service a human face. Use parent experiences that illuminate communication, care, or the sick-visit process.
- **Visual role and treatment** · Use a real provider portrait or approved team imagery as the focal point, followed by a contained review module. Keep the credentials useful even while Google assets are pending.
- **CTA and transitions** · Provide “Read all our Google reviews” when verified. Follow clinical proof with local proof by showing where the family will go.

### 5. Selected office and directions

- **Purpose and visitor question** · Make arrival predictable. “Where do I go, and when is this office open?”
- **Main message and content** · Identify the selected office, street address, office hours, and Morning Sick Call hours. Show a matching map and Get Directions link. List all three offices in the footer.
- **Emotion and trust** · Reduce the chance of a wasted trip. An accurate address, map, and schedule provide practical proof.
- **Visual role and treatment** · Pair the address and hours with a responsive map. Include an approved exterior or Google Business Profile photo of that office if it helps recognition.
- **CTA and transitions** · Put Get Directions below the map. After the practice and parent proof, resolve geography and then any remaining questions.

### 6. Practical FAQs

- **Purpose and visitor question** · Resolve objections before action. “What if I need a visit later, need help at night, or have a question about eligibility or coverage?”
- **Main message and content** · Prioritize check-in, afternoon or Saturday access, after-hours phone support, testing turnaround, eligibility once confirmed, and insurance. Keep emergency guidance visible earlier in the journey.
- **Emotion and trust** · Answer directly and acknowledge when a caregiver needs to confirm details by phone. Use clinic sources for coverage or scheduling claims.
- **Visual role and treatment** · Give this section a narrow, text-led layout. Use compact, accessible disclosure controls and short answers.
- **CTA and transitions** · Add a direct call link where calling is the documented resolution. Move from logistics to the final decision without introducing new claims.

### 7. Final CTA and footer

- **Purpose and visitor question** · Turn reassurance into a clear action. “What should I do now?”
- **Main message and content** · Briefly restate the selected office and sick-visit next step. Call leads. Request Appointment opens the same form as the earlier secondary CTA. Before the form, name the selected office and explain that the submission is a request and that calling is the route for time-sensitive inquiries.
- **Emotion and trust** · Close with reassurance about what happens next. Keep the selected office and contact details consistent through the action.
- **Visual role and treatment** · Use a high-contrast closing panel with one dominant call button. Keep the footer quieter and include all three offices, Privacy Policy, and Visit Our Website.
- **CTA and transitions** · Repeat Call and Request Appointment after the FAQs. The footer supplies navigation and practice details after the conversion point.
