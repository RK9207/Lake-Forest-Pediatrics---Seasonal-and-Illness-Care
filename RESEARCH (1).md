### 2.2 Conditions / problems addressed
- Seasonal illnesses named by the site: influenza (flu), RSV, strep throat, viral respiratory infections, mono and other seasonal viruses [VERIFIED S5].
- Colder months see more flu, RSV, strep and viral respiratory infections; warmer months more viral infections, allergies and GI illness [VERIFIED S5].
- General sick-visit scope: illnesses and injuries, from toddler falls to teen sports injuries [VERIFIED S2].
- **Colds:** Covered as a symptom-library topic [VERIFIED S18]. There is no "cold test"; do not imply testing for colds. Testing claims should be limited to flu, RSV, strep, mono (and urinalysis/lead per dated brochure).
- **COVID-19:** Appears only as a symptom-library topic [VERIFIED S18]. **No COVID testing claim found on the site.** Do not claim COVID testing without client confirmation.

### 2.3 Symptoms mentioned (by source)
**RSV symptoms (S3):** cough; low-grade fever; increased irritability; nasal congestion; runny nose; loss of appetite; difficulty breathing (often wheezing or abnormally fast breathing). RSV spreads person-to-person via droplets from sneezing/coughing. RSV is very common in young children but can affect any age [VERIFIED S3].

**Strep symptoms (S4):** sore throat; difficulty swallowing; enlarged, reddened tonsils; white patches on tongue or throat; fever; swollen neck lymph nodes; loss of appetite; stomach pain, nausea or vomiting; headache [VERIFIED S4].

**When to call the pediatrician (S5):** persistent or high fever; flu-like body aches and fatigue; severe sore throat or difficulty swallowing; ongoing cough or breathing concerns; unusual fatigue, dehydration or poor fluid intake; symptoms not improving after a few days [VERIFIED S5].

**When a child should generally be seen in the office (S2):** fever over 3 days (or any fever in an infant under 6 months); persistent vomiting or diarrhea; rashes; persistent pain (stomach, headache, ear pain, sore throat); any unusual symptom lasting more than 3 days [VERIFIED S2].

**Call 911 / go to the nearest ER (S2):** difficulty breathing (not just noisy or congested); extreme lethargy or difficulty staying awake; extreme irritability; dehydration; significant injury; severe pain not controlled by OTC pain medication; rectal temperature above 100.4 in an infant under 3 months [VERIFIED S2]. **This emergency guidance should appear on the page** as a safety disclaimer.

### 2.4 Benefits mentioned
- Early evaluation allows accurate diagnosis and timely treatment [VERIFIED S5].
- Rapid strep test results take only a few minutes, so treatment can be recommended right away instead of waiting days for lab results [VERIFIED S4].
- Rapid flu/RSV test options have a much faster turnaround (within a few hours) than lab-sent tests (typically a few days) [VERIFIED S3].
- Several flu tests can identify RSV from the same sample [VERIFIED S3].
- Pediatrician determines whether medication or lab testing is needed and discusses how to keep the child comfortable during recovery [VERIFIED S2].
- Guidance on managing symptoms at home [VERIFIED S5].
- Parents "do not need retail clinics, even after hours or over the weekend" [DATED S14] — positioning claim from the 2016-era brochure; use cautiously given current Sunday closure.
- One EHR across all locations, so the child's record is accessible whichever office or physician is used [DATED S14].

### 2.5 Treatment / service process
**Morning Sick Call process** [VERIFIED S6–S9 for the current one-line summary; details from DATED S17, 2021]:
1. Arrive at the office parking lot between 8 and 9 a.m. (current pages: "register on the website from the parking lot").
2. Open the website and tap "Morning Sick Call."
3. Enter child's name and date of birth, select the office, and give a reachable phone number; submit.
4. Staff monitor submissions in real time and call back [VERIFIED snippet S16]; nursing staff review symptoms by phone.
5. When ready, staff direct the family to the sick entrance door [DATED S17 — verify still current].
- First come, first served [DATED S17].
- Do not walk into the waiting room to join the queue; wait in the vehicle [DATED S17].

**Later in the day:** Call 847-295-1220; giving symptom details up front helps the team gauge severity and prepare the pediatrician [VERIFIED S2].

**Online:** Self-schedule a sick-illness appointment in the athenahealth patient portal; choose child, office and provider; slots limited [VERIFIED snippet S15]. Medication follow-ups, ADHD/mental-health follow-ups, extended illness appointments and extended wellness exams must be booked by phone [VERIFIED snippet S15].

**During the visit:** Pediatrician evaluates, decides on medication or lab testing, and advises on comfort/recovery [VERIFIED S2]. Rapid strep may be paired with a mono test; a negative rapid strep may be followed by a throat culture sent to a lab [VERIFIED S4].

**Unsure if a visit is needed:** Ask to speak with a patient educator, or use the online symptom checker [VERIFIED S2].

### 2.9 Limitations, caveats, disclaimers
- Morning sick call: Saturday only at Lake Bluff and Lindenhurst; Vernon Hills is Mon–Fri only [VERIFIED S2, S6, S9].

- Same-day is "when available" [VERIFIED S5]; online slots are limited [VERIFIED S15].
- Some mild flu/RSV cases resolve with rest; serious symptoms (breathing difficulty, high fever) need immediate attention [VERIFIED S3].
- Rapid strep can be negative while a throat culture is positive [VERIFIED S4].
- **Do NOT reuse:** S3 states flu and RSV "can be treated easily, though if left untreated will likely result in severe complications." This overstates outcomes (most RSV care is supportive; most cases are mild). Treat as a do-not-use claim.
- **Do NOT reuse:** "We're open 365 days a year" (S14, 2016 brochure). Offices are closed Sundays and holidays; the accurate current phrasing is "available 365 days a year, with medical professionals on call 24 hours a day."

- RSV looks like a common cold; few people recognize its early signs [VERIFIED S3].
- Mono and strep share symptoms, so they're often tested together [VERIFIED S4].
- Symptom checker and dosage charts available on site [VERIFIED S1 nav, S18].
